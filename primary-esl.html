Primary Checkpoint English as a Second Language

For learners at the end of Stage 6 who are learning English alongside another main language. Three short papers test reading, grammar and vocabulary, listening and writing.

Know what to expect

The papers

Each paper focuses on different skills. You write your answers on the question paper, so read every instruction and look at the example first.

Paper 1

Reading and Usage

35 minutes
  • Choose the correct word to complete sentences and short texts
  • Complete a conversation
  • Read short texts and answer questions about them
  • Tests grammar and vocabulary as well as reading
Paper 2

Listening

About 25 minutes
  • Short recordings: conversations, messages and announcements
  • Tick the right picture or answer, or fill in a missing word
  • You hear each recording twice
Paper 3

Writing

35 minutes
  • Short writing tasks, such as a message or email and a short story or description
  • Marked on content, organisation and accurate English
  • The task tells you what to include

Timings come from the Cambridge timetable. Your teacher will confirm the marks and details for your exam series. Older papers used the syllabus code 0837, and the skills they test are the same.

Where the marks are

How it's marked

Cambridge examiners mark your papers. Reading, usage and listening answers are right or wrong. Writing is marked on three things.

Reading, usage and listening

  • One mark for each correct answer
  • No marks are taken away for a wrong answer, so never leave a gap
  • Spelling matters when you copy a word from the text or write a word you hear

Writing

  • Content: did you include every point the task asked for?
  • Organisation: is it in a sensible order, with linking words such as and, but, because, then?
  • Language: are your grammar, vocabulary, spelling and punctuation correct, and do you use more than the simplest words?
Reading and usage

Question types and how to answer them

Know the question type before you start, and you will know exactly what to do.

Choose the right word

"She ___ to school every day. (go / goes / going)"

Read the whole sentence first. Look at the subject and the time words (every day, yesterday, now) to choose the right form.

Gap-fill text

"Elephants are ___ biggest land animals."

Read the whole text once before you fill any gap. The missing words are usually small grammar words: the, a, in, on, is, are, than, because.

Complete the conversation

A: "Would you like some juice?" B: "___"

Read what comes before and after the gap. The answer must make sense in both directions.

Reading for detail

"What time does the museum open?"

Underline the key words in the question, then scan the text for them. The answer is usually near those words.

True, false or doesn't say

"Tom has two sisters."

"False" means the text says the opposite. "Doesn't say" means the information is not there at all. Don't guess from what you know about the world.

Main idea

"What is the best title for this text?"

Think about the whole text, not one sentence. The first and last sentences often give the main idea.

Matching

"Match each person to the club they should join."

Read what each person wants, then look for the same idea in different words. Cross out each option when you have used it.

Listening: choose the picture

"What will Anna wear to the party?"

Look at all the pictures before you listen and name what is different in each one. The speakers often mention all of them, so wait for the final decision.

Listening: fill the gap

"The trip is on ___."

Before you listen, decide what kind of word is missing: a day, a number, a place or a name. Write it the first time and check spelling the second time.

Paper 2 practice

Listening practice

Read the questions first, then press play. Listen twice, just like in the exam, before you open the script.

1 · At the school library

A conversation between Sara and the librarian

How many books can Sara borrow at one time?

The librarian says, "You can take three at a time." Five is the number Sara asks about.

Which book does Sara take in the end?

The space book is already out and she has read the horse book, so she chooses volcanoes.

When must Sara bring the book back?

The librarian says, "Please bring it back on Friday."

Show the script

Sara: Hello, Miss Hall. Can I borrow five books today, please?

Librarian: Hello, Sara. I'm sorry, not five. You can take three at a time.

Sara: Oh, OK. Have you got the book about space?

Librarian: Another student has it at the moment. What about this one, about horses?

Sara: I read that last month. Oh, look, volcanoes! I'll take this one.

Librarian: Good choice. Please bring it back on Friday.

2 · The class trip

A teacher is talking to her class

Where is the class going?

The teacher says, "We are going to the science museum."

What time does the bus leave?

Students must arrive by eight o'clock, but the bus leaves at quarter past eight.

What do the students NOT need to bring?

The teacher says, "You don't need any money. The tickets are already paid for."

Show the script

Teacher: Good morning, everyone. Please listen carefully. On Thursday, we are going to the science museum.

Please be at school by eight o'clock. The bus leaves at quarter past eight, and it will not wait.

Bring a packed lunch and a bottle of water. You don't need any money. The tickets are already paid for.

And please wear your school uniform, so that I can find you easily.

3 · Plans for Saturday

Two friends, Mina and Zoe, are talking on the phone

What do the girls decide to do?

The pool is closed and Zoe doesn't like tennis, so they choose the cinema.

What time will they meet?

The film starts at three, so they agree to meet at half past two to buy tickets.

Where will they meet?

Zoe says, "Let's meet at the bus stop near your house."

Show the script

Mina: Hi Zoe! Do you want to go swimming on Saturday?

Zoe: I'd love to, but the pool is closed this week.

Mina: Oh no. We could play tennis in the park.

Zoe: Hmm, I don't really like tennis. How about the cinema? There's a new film about a dog.

Mina: Great idea! The film starts at three o'clock.

Zoe: Then let's meet at half past two to buy the tickets. Let's meet at the bus stop near your house.

Mina: Perfect. See you on Saturday!

Paper 3

Writing guide

Short writing tasks have clear rules. Do everything the task asks, keep to the number of words, and check your verbs.

Short message or noteTo a friend or family member

Features

  • Start with Hi or Hello and the name
  • Answer every point in the task. There are usually three
  • Short, clear sentences
  • Finish with your name

Useful phrases

  • Would you like to…?
  • Can you bring…?
  • Let's meet at…
  • See you soon!

ModelHi Lena, would you like to come to my house on Saturday? We can play board games and make pizza. Please bring your new game. See you soon! Farah

EmailGiving news or answering questions

Features

  • A greeting: Hi Sam, or Dear Mrs Lee,
  • A friendly first line: Thank you for your email.
  • One or two sentences for each question you must answer
  • An ending: Write soon, or Best wishes,

Make it better

  • Join ideas with and, but, because, so
  • Add one detail to each answer
  • Use the past for what you did and "going to" for plans

ModelHi Sam, thank you for your email. Last weekend I went to the beach with my family. We swam in the sea and ate ice cream because it was very hot. Next weekend I am going to visit my grandmother. Write soon, Karim

Short storyFrom pictures or a first sentence

Features

  • A beginning, a middle and an end
  • Past tense all the way through
  • Time words: first, then, after that, suddenly, finally
  • Say how the people felt

If there are pictures

  • Write one or two sentences for each picture
  • Give the people names
  • Describe what you can see: the weather, the place, the faces

ModelLast Sunday, Ben and his sister went to the park. First, they played football. Suddenly, the ball flew into a tree! Ben climbed up carefully and got it. Finally, they went home, tired but happy.

DescriptionA person, a place or your day

Features

  • Present simple for things that are always true
  • Adjectives before the noun: a tall, friendly teacher
  • "There is" and "There are" for places
  • Say what you like about it, and why

Useful phrases

  • My favourite place is…
  • It has got…
  • I like it because…
  • The best thing about it is…

ModelMy favourite place is my grandmother's garden. There are tall orange trees and a small green pond. I like it because it is quiet, and the best thing about it is the swing.

Exam technique

Exam tips

Six habits that win marks. Press the speaker on any tip to hear it read aloud.

01

Read the example first

Every part of the paper begins with an example. Look at it carefully, because it shows you exactly how to answer.

02

Look for time words

Words like yesterday, now, every day and tomorrow tell you which tense to use. Circle them before you choose your answer.

03

Never leave a gap

You do not lose marks for a wrong answer. If you are not sure, cross out the answers you know are wrong and choose the best one left.

04

Listen to the end

Speakers often change their minds. Wait until the end of the recording before you choose, and use the second listening to check.

05

Answer every point

In the writing paper, the task tells you what to include. Tick each point when you have written about it.

06

Check your verbs

At the end, check every verb. Is it the right tense, and does it match the subject? He goes, they go.
