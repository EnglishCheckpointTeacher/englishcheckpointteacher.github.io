Lower Secondary Checkpoint English as a Second Language

For learners at the end of Stage 9 who are learning English alongside another main language. Three papers test reading, grammar and vocabulary, listening and writing.

Know what to expect

The papers

You answer on the question paper. Each part begins with instructions and an example, so read them before you start.

Paper 1

Reading and Usage

45 minutes
  • Grammar and vocabulary: choose or write the correct word
  • Word formation and sentence completion
  • Reading comprehension on longer texts: detail, opinion and main ideas
  • Matching people or statements to texts
Paper 2

Listening

About 35 minutes
  • Several parts, from short dialogues to longer talks and interviews
  • Listen for specific details, opinions, attitudes and main ideas
  • Multiple choice and note completion
  • You hear each recording twice
Paper 3

Writing

40 minutes
  • Usually a shorter task, such as an email, and a longer one, such as an article, story or review
  • Each task gives you points you must cover and a word guide
  • Marked on content, organisation and range and accuracy of language

Where the marks are

How it's marked

Cambridge examiners mark your papers. Reading, usage and listening answers are right or wrong. Writing is judged on three things.

Reading, usage and listening

  • One mark for each correct answer
  • No marks are taken away for wrong answers, so answer everything
  • In word formation and note completion, the spelling must be correct
  • Keep to the word limit: "no more than three words" means three or fewer

Writing

  • Content: every point in the task is covered and developed, in the right tone for the reader
  • Organisation: clear paragraphs, a logical order and a range of linking words
  • Language: a range of tenses, structures and vocabulary, used accurately

What this means for you: a short, simple answer with no mistakes will not reach the top marks. Examiners reward range, so show a second conditional, a passive or a relative clause when it fits.

Reading, usage and listening

Question types and how to answer them

Each task type has a method. Practise the method until it is automatic.

Grammar multiple choice

"If I ___ more time, I would travel. (have / had / will have)"

Identify the structure first: a conditional, a passive, a tense. Then check that the word agrees with everything around it.

Open gap-fill

"She has lived here ___ 2019."

No options are given. The missing word is nearly always a grammar word: a preposition, article, auxiliary verb, pronoun or linker. Write one word only.

Word formation

"The film was very ___. (ENTERTAIN)"

Decide which part of speech the gap needs (noun, adjective, adverb, verb), then add the right ending. Check whether it should be negative or plural.

Sentence transformation

"They built the bridge in 1990." → "The bridge ___ in 1990."

The second sentence must mean the same as the first. Common changes: active to passive, direct to reported speech, "too" to "not enough".

Reading for detail and opinion

"What does the writer think about online learning?"

Words like "but", "however" and "actually" often signal the writer's real opinion. Wrong options repeat words from the text with a different meaning.

Matching

"Which person mentions saving money?"

Look for the same idea in different words (paraphrase), not the same word. Do the easy matches first.

Missing sentences

"Choose the sentence that fits each gap."

Look at the words just before and after the gap. Pronouns (this, they, it) and linkers (however, as a result) show you what must come next.

Listening: multiple choice

"Why did Maya change her mind?"

Read the question and underline the key word. All three options are usually mentioned. Only one answers the question that was asked.

Listening: note completion

"The club meets every ___ evening."

Predict the type of answer (a day, a number, a place) before you listen. Write exactly what you hear and check spelling on the second listening.

Paper 2 practice

Listening practice

Read the questions first, then press play. Listen twice, as in the exam, before you open the script.

1 · The history project

Two students, Hana and Priya, are planning a presentation

Which topic do the girls finally choose?

Three other groups are doing Egypt, and Priya thinks the Olympics is too big a topic. They agree on flight.

Why does Hana not want to make a poster?

Hana says nobody at the back would be able to read it. Time is mentioned, but as a reason to start soon.

When will they meet to practise?

Priya has music on Tuesday and the presentation itself is on Thursday, so they choose Wednesday lunchtime.

Show the script

Hana: So, we need a topic for the history presentation. I was thinking of Ancient Egypt.

Priya: I like it, but three other groups are already doing Egypt. What about the Olympic Games?

Hana: Hmm. That's interesting, although isn't it too big? We've only got five minutes.

Priya: True. Actually, my uncle is a pilot. We could do the history of flight, and I could borrow some of his photos.

Hana: Perfect, let's do that. Shall we make slides? I'd rather not do a poster, because nobody at the back would be able to read it.

Priya: Slides are fine. We haven't got much time, so we should practise soon. I've got music on Tuesday after school, though.

Hana: And the presentation is on Thursday morning. How about Wednesday at lunchtime?

Priya: Wednesday lunchtime works for me. See you in the library.

2 · Photography competition

An announcement on a school radio programme

What is the theme of this year's competition?

"Wild animals" was last year's theme. This year it is "My neighbourhood".

What is the closing date?

The speaker says "the thirtieth of March", then spells it out as "three zero". Thirteen and thirty are a classic listening trap.

What will the winner receive?

The winner gets a camera. The second and third prizes are book tokens, and all the best photos will be shown in the town gallery.

How should students enter?

The speaker says printed photos cannot be accepted this year. Entries must be uploaded to the school website.

Show the script

Presenter: And now, some exciting news for anyone who loves taking pictures. The school photography competition is back.

Last year's theme was wild animals, and we received more than two hundred entries. This year, the theme is "My neighbourhood". We want to see the streets, the people and the places that make your area special.

The closing date is the thirtieth of March. That's three zero, so you have plenty of time.

The winner will receive a camera, and there are book tokens for second and third place. The twenty best photographs will also be displayed in the town gallery during April.

Please note that we cannot accept printed photos this year. To enter, upload your photo to the school website and write one sentence explaining why you took it. Good luck!

3 · The repair café

An interview with Leila, a student who started a repair club

Why did Leila start the repair café?

She says seeing a nearly new bicycle at the rubbish tip "really shocked" her. The café is free.

What surprised her most?

She expected the repairs to be the main thing, but "what I hadn't expected" was grandparents and teenagers teaching each other.

What is Leila's attitude to the future of the café?

She would love every school to have one, but admits they need more volunteers first.

Show the script

Interviewer: Today I'm talking to Leila, who is fifteen and runs a repair café at her school. Leila, what gave you the idea?

Leila: Well, last year I went to the rubbish tip with my mum, and I saw a bicycle that was nearly new. It only needed a new chain. That really shocked me. I thought, if people knew how to fix things, they wouldn't throw so much away.

Interviewer: So how does the café work? Do people pay?

Leila: No, it's completely free. Once a month, people bring broken things, like lamps, toys or clothes, and volunteers help them to repair them.

Interviewer: Has anything surprised you?

Leila: I thought the repairs would be the most important part. What I hadn't expected was how much the older and younger people would enjoy working together. Grandparents teach us to sew, and we show them how to fix their phones.

Interviewer: And what's next?

Leila: I'd love every school in the city to have one. To be honest, we'll need a lot more volunteers before that can happen, but I think we'll get there.

Paper 3

Writing guide

Cover every point in the task, choose the right tone for your reader, and show a range of accurate English.

Informal emailTo a friend or family member

Features

  • Hi / Hello + name
  • A friendly opening line: "It was great to hear from you!"
  • One short paragraph for each point in the task
  • Contractions are fine: I'm, can't, we'll
  • Close with "Write soon," or "Best wishes," and your name

Show your range

  • Mix tenses: I've just…, I'm going to…, Last week I…
  • Linkers: although, as well as, so that
  • One or two less common words: thrilled, unforgettable

ModelHi Daniel, it was great to hear about your new school! I've just joined a photography club, and although it's only been two weeks, I've already learnt so much. Next month we're going to visit the old harbour to take pictures of the sunrise. Write soon, Yara

Semi-formal emailTo a teacher, club or organisation

Features

  • Dear Mr / Ms + surname
  • State your reason for writing first
  • Polite requests: "Could you…?", "I would be grateful if…"
  • No slang, fewer contractions
  • End with "Kind regards," and your full name

Useful phrases

  • I am writing to ask about…
  • I would like to know whether…
  • Thank you in advance for your help.

Model openingDear Ms Okafor, I am writing to ask about the summer science camp advertised on the school website. Could you tell me whether students need to bring their own equipment?

Article or blog postFor a school magazine or website

Features

  • A title that makes people want to read
  • An opening question or surprising fact
  • Two or three paragraphs, each with one main idea
  • Your own opinion, with reasons and an example
  • A final line that speaks to the reader

Useful phrases

  • Have you ever wondered…?
  • One of the main reasons is…
  • In my opinion, … / As far as I'm concerned, …
  • Why not give it a try?

Model openingHave you ever spent a whole day without looking at a screen? Last month our class tried it, and the result was not what any of us expected.

StoryFrom a title or a first sentence

Features

  • If a first sentence is given, start with it exactly
  • A clear beginning, problem and ending
  • Narrative tenses: past simple, past continuous, past perfect
  • Time linkers: as soon as, meanwhile, by the time, eventually
  • Feelings and one line of direct speech

Show your range

  • "I was walking home when I heard…"
  • "I had never seen anything like it."
  • Adverbs: nervously, immediately, luckily

Model openingWhen I opened the door, I couldn't believe my eyes. My little sister was standing on the kitchen table, and she had covered every wall with paintings of our cat.

ReviewOf a film, book, game or place

Features

  • Name what you are reviewing and what kind of thing it is
  • A short summary, without giving away the ending
  • What you liked and what could be better
  • A clear recommendation: who would enjoy it?

Useful phrases

  • It is set in… / It tells the story of…
  • What I liked most was…
  • The only disappointing thing was…
  • I would definitely recommend it to anyone who…

Model opening"The Last Lighthouse" is an adventure novel set on a tiny island off the coast of Scotland. It tells the story of two cousins who discover that the lighthouse keeper has disappeared.

A model answer, with notes

Task: Your English friend Sophie wants to know about a festival in your country. Write an email. Say when it happens, what people do, and why you enjoy it.

Hi Sophie,
Thanks for your email. It was lovely to hear from you!1

You asked about festivals. My favourite is the spring festival, which takes place every year at the beginning of April.2

On that day, families get up early and have a picnic by the river. Traditional food is prepared the night before,3 and children paint eggs in bright colours.

I love it because it's the one day when my whole family is together. If you came in April, you could join us!4

Write soon,
Nour5

Why it earns marks

  1. The right tone for a friend: an informal greeting and a warm first line.
  2. Point one (when) is answered clearly, with a relative clause.
  3. Point two (what) uses a passive. That is range.
  4. Point three (why) gives a reason, then adds a second conditional.
  5. A proper informal ending. Every point has its own short paragraph.

Exam technique

Exam tips

Six habits that win marks. Press the speaker on any tip to hear it read aloud.

01

Watch your time

Paper 1 has several parts. If a question is taking too long, make your best guess, mark it, and come back at the end.

02

Look for paraphrase

Correct answers usually say the same idea in different words. If an option copies words from the text exactly, check it carefully.

03

Learn word families

For every new word, learn its family: decide, decision, decisive, decisively. Word formation questions test exactly this.

04

Predict before listening

Read the questions before each recording and guess what kind of answer you need: a time, a name, a number or a reason.

05

Beware of distractors

In listening, speakers often mention one answer and then change it. Let's meet at six. Actually, no, half past six. Listen to the end.

06

Count the points

A writing task usually gives you three points to cover. Write one short paragraph for each, so the examiner can see them all.
